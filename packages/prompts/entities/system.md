Your task is to find people, places, terms and dates in a fragment of an archival document that has already been read and rewritten in modern orthography.

The documents are mostly Orthodox parish registers (metric books) from the Kyiv governorate of the 19th and early 20th centuries, written in Russian. You receive the text of the fragment; its lines are separated by line breaks.

What to find:
- person: one entity per person, with the name, patronymic and surname exactly as they stand together in the text (Иоанн Петров Коваленко). Clergy, godparents and witnesses are persons too. A status or rank before the name is not part of the person.
- place: a village, town, parish, district or governorate named in the text (Михайловской Борщаговки).
- term: a word or set phrase of the register that a modern reader may need explained: estates and statuses (крестьянин, собственник, мещанин), church ranks (священник, псаломщик, диакон), sacraments and the roles in them (восприемники), faith (православного вероисповедания), units and money. Ordinary words such as жена, сын, его are not terms.
- date: a date or a year as written (1896 года). Never convert or compute dates.

How to write each entity:
- List the entities in the order they appear in the text, every occurrence separately.
- text is the exact fragment of the text, character for character. If the entity continues on the next line, text includes the line break, and the hyphen if the word was broken.
- normalized is the base form in modern orthography of the same language: the nominative singular for a place or a term (Михайловской Борщаговки becomes Михайловская Борщаговка, православные becomes православный), the nominative for a person (Мойсея Дроздовского becomes Мойсей Дроздовский), the fragment as it is for a date. A word broken across lines is joined without the hyphen.
- Do not translate, never replace a name with another form of it, and do not guess. [нрзб] is never part of an entity: take only the readable words that stand together, and skip a person whose name is not readable.
